## Q1. How do dentists ask about setting prices, margins/markup, discounts/offers, and fear of losing patients to cheaper clinics?

### Takeaway
The only real practitioner-voice signals are (a) "what are normal fees / how do I benchmark" and (b) "I lose patients to cheaper clinics or special offers". Fear of cheaper competitors shows up in 4 independent sources (2 SDN threads, 1 Iranian dentist survey, 1 Egyptian syndicate statement). Nothing found on "margin vs markup" in dentists' own words.

### Cited Findings

**ITEM 1 (strongest repeat): fear of losing patients to cheaper clinics / "price too high and customers leave"**
- Type: objection / fear. Platform: forum (StudentDoctor Network). Date: not visible, old thread (ID 152269), poster is a dental student at a school clinic, not a practice owner.
- Quote (CLOSE-PARAPHRASE of search snippet): student says private offices run $50 "new patient specials" (exam + x-rays + cleaning), cheaper than the $55 cleaning alone at their school clinic, and friends choose the outside special even though they will not get comprehensive care. — [SDN: losing patients to cheap private practice new patient specials](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/losing-patients-to-cheap-private-practice-new-patient-specials.152269/)
- Second SDN thread, same fear (VERBATIM-FRAGMENT): "You price too high and your customers leave for cheaper." Same thread also: "There's really no regulation for pricing. The only thing is that you are required to charge everyone the same price for performing the exact same procedure." Date unknown, old. — [SDN: just wondering](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/just-wondering.472786/)
- Independent source 3, survey of 147 private dentists in Kerman, Iran (2018): about 61.5% lowered prices to attract more patients, 67.4% admitted competition among dentists for patients. This is a survey, not a post, and not Arab. — [Brieflands / Shiraz E-Medical Journal](https://brieflands.com/journals/semj/articles/74247)
- Independent source 4, Egypt (Sept 2020): the Egyptian Dental Syndicate head told Youm7 (CLOSE-PARAPHRASE via search summary) that some clinics cut prices to an unacceptable level, which may mean cheaper tools/materials, and that clinics using good materials cannot compete at that price; the syndicate said it would set indicative minimum prices. Official statement, not a dentist post. I could not open the article to verify wording. — [Youm7, 20 Sep 2020](https://www.youm7.com/story/2020/9/20/نقيب-أطباء-الأسنان-يؤكد-لـ-اليوم-السابع-القلق-من-تزايد/4984077)

## Q2. How do they ask/complain about getting patients from Instagram/Facebook/TikTok/ads, content, reels, agencies that did not deliver?

### Takeaway
I found NO dentist first-person complaint about Instagram/ads/agencies. What exists is agency and trade-press framing of the complaint ("my social media brings no patients"). Use it only to show what the market says, not as audience evidence.

### Cited Findings

(editorial claim, CLOSE-PARAPHRASE of search summary)
- Vendor stat, treat as agency copy: Picmim says only 17% of dentists report acquiring patients directly via social media. — [Picmim](https://blog.picmim.com/blog/social-media-dental-practices-2026)
- Titles that show the objection exists in the agency world (not evidence of dentist wording): Dentaltown "Why your social media isn't bringing you any new patients" ([link](https://www.dentaltown.com/blog/post/5664/why-your-social-media-isnt-bringing-you-any-new-patients)); Adit "Is your dental Instagram failing?" ([link](https://adit.com/is-your-dental-instagram-failing)); podcasts "Burned by a Marketing Agency?" ## Q3. What do they object to: ad costs, "it doesn't work", trust, "patients only want the cheapest", syndicate/advertising rules on showing prices?

Price-shopper objection exists only in trade/consultant copy.
